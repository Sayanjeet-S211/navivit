# NAVI: Navigation Assistant for Visitor Interaction
**Autonomous Campus Guidance Quadcopter — System Documentation & Web Showcase Specification**[span_0](start_span)[span_0](end_span)

## 1. Project Overview & Scope

* **System Concept:** NAVI is an autonomous micro aerial vehicle (quadcopter) designed to guide campus visitors and parents directly to their destinations across the Vellore Institute of Technology (VIT), Chennai campus[span_1](start_span)[span_1](end_span)[span_2](start_span)[span_2](end_span).
* **Core Problem Statement:** Physical campus signage is passive and requires self-localization, student/staff guides do not scale during peak event days, and commercial mobile navigation apps lack internal path resolution[span_3](start_span)[span_3](end_span)[span_4](start_span)[span_4](end_span).
* **Navigation Mechanism:** NAVI computes a route over a surveyed campus path graph, takes off from a launch pad, flies ahead of the visitor at a safe lead distance, waits when they fall behind, and lands at a pad near their destination[span_5](start_span)[span_5](end_span)[span_6](start_span)[span_6](end_span).
* **In-Scope Boundaries:** Autonomous outdoor daylight flights in fair weather across 36 mapped campus destinations, partitioned into single flight legs of at most 350 m[span_7](start_span)[span_7](end_span).
* **Out-of-Scope Elements:** Indoor flight, night or bad-weather operations, simultaneous multi-visitor guidance, autonomous charging docks, physical payload delivery, and dynamic obstacle re-routing[span_8](start_span)[span_8](end_span).
* **Core Technical Contribution:** Coupling an autonomous aircraft to the walking pace of an uninstrumented human via a ground-based pacing controller, combined with graph routing, set-cover launch pad optimization, and geofencing[span_9](start_span)[span_9](end_span).
* **Institutional Background:** Interdisciplinary initiative developed across SMEC (Mechanical), SENSE (Electronics), and SCOPE (Computer Science) at VIT Chennai[span_10](start_span)[span_10](end_span).
* **Project Budget:** Self-funded pool of Rs 25,000 contributed equally by five team members (Rs 5,000 each)[span_11](start_span)[span_11](end_span)[span_12](start_span)[span_12](end_span).

### Key Hardware Design Decisions
* **Zero Onboard Companion Computer:** Path planning, graph traversal, and pacing algorithms run on the ground station laptop or kiosk, sending commands via MAVLink[span_51](start_span)[span_51](end_span). This eliminates payload weight, lowers cost, and maximizes battery life[span_52](start_span)[span_52](end_span).
* **Rejection of Proprietary / Closed Drones:** Commercial camera drones were rejected because their closed firmware prohibits external MAVLink control and their follow-me modes follow rather than lead[span_53](start_span)[span_53](end_span)[span_54](start_span)[span_54](end_span).
* **Rejection of Legacy Boards:** KK2.1.5 and APM 2.8 boards were excluded due to lack of autonomous GPS support and modern ArduPilot compatibility[span_55](start_span)[span_55](end_span)[span_56](start_span)[span_56](end_span).
* **Standard GPS Adaptation:** Standard GPS delivers 2.5–5 m positioning accuracy rather than centimeter-level RTK precision[span_57](start_span)[span_57](end_span)[span_58](start_span)[span_58](end_span). The system compensates by setting the lead distance to ~15 m and establishing $5\text{ m} \times 5\text{ m}$ clear landing zones at pads[span_59](start_span)[span_59](end_span)[span_60](start_span)[span_60](end_span).

## 3. Web Showcase & Interactive CAD Exploded View Specification

The web showcase highlights the physical quadcopter design, internal wiring, and real-time navigation telemetry through an interactive interface[span_61](start_span)[span_61](end_span)[span_62](start_span)[span_62](end_span)[span_63](start_span)[span_63](end_span).

### A. 3D Exploded CAD Canvas & Controls
* **Linear Explosion Slider:** An interactive range slider (0% to 100%) that expands the CAD model outward along defined axis vectors.
* **Subsystem Isolation Layers:**
  * *Structural Layer:* Glass-fiber top plate, bottom plate with integrated PDB copper traces, arms, and tall landing legs[span_64](start_span)[span_64](end_span)[span_65](start_span)[span_65](end_span).
  * *Avionics Layer:* F405 flight controller isolated on M3 rubber anti-vibration grommets to protect the IMU from motor vibrations[span_66](start_span)[span_66](end_span)[span_67](start_span)[span_67](end_span)[span_68](start_span)[span_68](end_span).
  * *Propulsion Layer:* A2212 1000KV brushless motors, 30A SimonK ESCs, and 1045 propellers[span_69](start_span)[span_69](end_span)[span_70](start_span)[span_70](end_span).
  * *Sensor Mast & Telemetry Layer:* Raised GPS/compass mast, 433 MHz SiK transceiver antenna, and FlySky iA6B iBUS receiver[span_71](start_span)[span_71](end_span)[span_72](start_span)[span_72](end_span).
  * *Power Bay:* 3S LiPo battery pack mounted centrally with XT60 wiring and current-sensing power module[span_73](start_span)[span_73](end_span)[span_74](start_span)[span_74](end_span).
* **Interactive Hotspots:** Clickable 3D markers pinned to components displaying specifications, UART pinouts, and budget allocations[span_75](start_span)[span_75](end_span)[span_76](start_span)[span_76](end_span).
* **Scrollytelling Inspection:** Smooth camera panning that guides visitors through the airframe, electronics stack, and sensor mast during page scrolling.

### B. Real-Time Telemetry & Ground Station HUD
* **Flight State Badges:** Live status chips indicating active flight modes (`AUTO`, `GUIDED`, `LOITER`, `RTL`, `MANUAL`)[span_77](start_span)[span_77](end_span)[span_78](start_span)[span_78](end_span).
* **Synthetic HUD Readouts:** Virtual instrument gauges displaying cruising altitude (~15 m AGL), ground speed, battery voltage, current draw, and GPS HDOP/satellite count[span_79](start_span)[span_79](end_span)[span_80](start_span)[span_80](end_span)[span_81](start_span)[span_81](end_span)[span_82](start_span)[span_82](end_span).
